# Listening test structure and background

This page presents the thought behind the conducted listening test in which test participants rated pairs of sounds based on similarity.

Some of the experiment is based on the paper [Spectral and Temporal Cues for perception of material and action categories in impacted sound sources](https://kbdk-aub.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/discovery/fulldisplay?context=PC&vid=45KBDK_AUB:AUB&search_scope=MyInst_and_CI&tab=Everything&lang=da&docid=cdi_scitation_primary_10_1121_1_4955181) by Hjortkjær and McAdams.


## Subjects
Subjects were required to be over the age of 18 and consented to some of their personal details to be stored by the data controller.
Section further describing the details of the test participants


## Stimuli
The test stimuli consists of two different players recording single drum impulses. Two different strokes were recorded: A normal and a controlled version. 
For the controlled stroked, the player was instructed to return the drumstrick to the same position for every stroke, whereas for the normal stroke this position could vary. 

In total eight sounds with these variations were chosen, meaning two normal strokes and two controlled strokes for each player (giving eight total sounds).



## Procedure
The subjects were instructed to sit in a relatively quiet space and to preferably use closed headphones to reduce background noise. 

The stimuli were presented to the subjects in random pairs in a survey, with their corresponding similarity answers given by a slider scale with "Very Similar" and "Very Dissimilar" at the extremes.

A pre-test with 5 questions were included to give the subjects time to set the volume to a comfortable level. Furthermore the subjects were encouraged to use the full range of the slider scale, to distinct between similar and dissimilar drum strokes.

For the main experiment, all combinations of the eight drum strokes were presented to the subjects, meaning 36 questions in the survey was needed. 
This also means that eight of the questions were presenting a repeated sound (two identical stimuli). These were also spread out randomly and were included as a test, to see if the subjects were able to tell a distinct similarity.
