# Listening test structure and background

This page presents the thought behind the conducted listening test in which test participants rated pairs of sounds based on similarity.

Some of the experiment is based on the paper [Spectral and Temporal Cues](https://kbdk-aub.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/discovery/fulldisplay?context=PC&vid=45KBDK_AUB:AUB&search_scope=MyInst_and_CI&tab=Everything&lang=da&docid=cdi_scitation_primary_10_1121_1_4955181) by Hjortkjær and McAdams.


## Subjects
Subjects were required to be over the age of 18 and consented to some of their personal details to be stored by the data controller.
Section further describing the details of the test participants


## Stimuli
The test stimuli consists of two different players recording single drum impulses. Two different strokes were recorded: A normal and a controlled version. 
For the controlled stroked, the player was instructed to return the drumstrick to the same position for every stroke, whereas for the normal stroke this position could vary. 

In total eight sounds with these variations were chosen, meaning two normal strokes and two controlled strokes for each player (giving eight total sounds).



## Procedure
The sounds were presented to the listener in pairs, meaning for all combinations of the sounds to occur, 36 questions in the survey was needed. 
Furthermore four extra questions presenting some of the sounds together with itself were included as a test, to see if the subjects were able to tell a distinct similarity.
